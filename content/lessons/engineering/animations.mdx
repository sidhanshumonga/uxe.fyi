Animation explains change: where something came from, where it went, what just happened. Good animation is quick and purposeful; most users won't notice it, only that the interface feels clear.

## Pick the right tool

- **CSS transitions** — for changes between two states, like hover or open/closed.
- **CSS keyframes** — for looping or multi-step motion, like a spinner or a shimmer.
- **Web Animations API** — `element.animate()` gives you keyframes from JavaScript, with the ability to pause, reverse and await `finished`.
- **Springs** — for motion that should feel physical or respond to gestures. Libraries like [Motion](/resources/motion) provide spring physics.

## Animate cheap properties

`transform` and `opacity` can usually be animated by the compositor without recalculating layout. Animating `width`, `height`, `top` or `left` forces layout on every frame and is much more likely to stutter. To animate size or position changes smoothly, measure, then animate a transform (the FLIP technique).

## Timing and easing

Most interface transitions sit between about 150 and 300ms. Entering elements should ease out (arrive fast, settle gently); exiting elements can ease in. Compare the curves:

## Reduced motion

Some people get dizzy or distracted from motion. Honour `prefers-reduced-motion` by removing large movement and parallax, while keeping quick fades that still explain the change.

```css
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  .panel { transition: opacity 150ms linear; transform: none; }
}
```
