Product managers own what gets built and when. UX engineers care about how it feels. The two goals rarely conflict, but they need translating to each other, and that translation is a big part of the job.

## Speak in outcomes

"The easing is wrong" is taste. "This panel feels slow, so people will think the app is laggy" is an outcome a PM can weigh. Tie craft to things they care about: conversion, support tickets, retention, trust in a sensitive flow.

## Offer options, not vetoes

Instead of "we can't ship this", offer a menu:

- **Ship as specified** — and accept the known rough edges
- **Small fix** — half a day, covers the most common case
- **Proper fix** — two days, covers the edge cases too

Options turn a disagreement into a decision you make together.

## Plan polish early

Polish added at the end looks like scope creep. Polish planned at the start is part of the estimate. When a feature kicks off, name the few details that will matter most (loading states, the error path, a key transition) and include them in the plan.

## Show, don't argue

A quick [coded prototype](/learning/prototyping-in-code) or a before/after recording settles most debates about feel faster than a discussion. Let people feel the difference.

## Pick your battles

Not every detail is worth a fight. Save your influence for the moments where users are most affected, and let the small things go when the deadline is real.
