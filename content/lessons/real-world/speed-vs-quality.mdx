There's never time to polish everything. The skill is knowing which details matter now, which can wait, and which can be skipped entirely, and being deliberate about it.

## Reversible vs expensive

Some decisions are cheap to change later: copy, colours, the order of a menu, a transition's duration. Ship those fast and adjust with real usage. Others are expensive: data models, URLs, public APIs, component contracts used across a codebase. Slow down on those.

## Where polish pays

Polish has the most value where many people pass through and where trust is at stake:

- Sign-up, onboarding and the core task
- Payments, deletes and anything with data at risk
- Errors and recovery paths

Low-traffic admin screens and internal tools can stay plain.

## A quality floor

Shipping rough doesn't mean shipping broken. Agree on a floor that every release meets regardless of deadline: it works with a keyboard, has visible focus and sufficient contrast, handles errors without losing data, and doesn't shift layout. Above the floor, trade freely.

## Write down what you skipped

When you deliberately leave something rough, note it in an issue or the PR description. Conscious debt is easy to pay back later; forgotten debt becomes the way things are.

## Revisit

Polish what real usage shows matters. Analytics, support tickets and watching people use the product will tell you which rough edges people actually hit.
