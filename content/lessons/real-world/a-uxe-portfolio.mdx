A UX engineering portfolio has to show something screenshots can't: how things move, respond and fail, and the judgement behind them. Hiring managers want to see how you think as much as what you built.

## Interaction, constraint, decision

For each project, tell a short story in three parts:

- **The interaction** — what the user does and what should happen
- **The constraint** — what made it hard: performance, accessibility, an API, a deadline
- **The decision** — what you chose, what you rejected, and why

This structure shows both craft and judgement, and it's easy for a reader to skim.

## Make it playable

Interaction work is best shown live. Embed a working demo, link a coded prototype, or at least include a short screen recording. A demo people can poke at is more convincing than any description of it. The [breakdowns](/breakdowns) on this site are one format to borrow.

## Depth over breadth

Three projects explained well beat ten thumbnails. Choose work that shows range: one systems piece, one detailed interaction, one where you worked closely with design or product.

## Show the details

Include the states most people skip: loading, errors, empty states, keyboard behaviour, reduced motion. Showing that you thought about them signals exactly the kind of care the role needs.

## Be honest about your part

Say clearly what you did on team projects. Credit collaborators. It reads as confidence, and interviewers will ask anyway.
