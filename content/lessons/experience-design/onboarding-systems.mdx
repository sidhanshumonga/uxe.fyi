Onboarding isn't a tour. It's everything between sign-up and the first moment a person gets value from the product, and the job is to make that path short.

## First value

Identify the first meaningful outcome: the first document shared, the first deploy, the first report. Then remove or postpone everything that doesn't lead there. Settings, profile details and integrations can almost always wait.

## Progressive disclosure

Show the essentials first and reveal advanced features as people need them. A new user sees a simple surface; power features appear in context, the first time they'd be useful. This keeps the signal-to-noise ratio high for beginners without hiding depth from experts.

## Empty states and defaults

The first screen most new users see is empty. A good [empty state](/breakdowns/empty-states) says what will appear, sets the tone, and offers one next step, such as a sample to explore. Sensible defaults mean a new user gets a reasonable result without configuring anything.

## Tours and tooltips

Product tours are easy to add and easy to skip, and much of what they explain is forgotten as soon as they end. Prefer teaching in context: a hint that appears the first time a feature is relevant, dismissed for good once used.

## Measure it

Track how many new users reach first value and how long it takes. Watching a few real people sign up will show you more than any funnel chart.
