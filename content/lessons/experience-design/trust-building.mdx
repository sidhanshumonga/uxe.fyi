Users decide whether to trust a product in a few specific moments: when they hand over money or data, delete something, or hit an error. Every small promise kept in those moments adds up.

## Predictable actions

Before a consequential action, users should know exactly what will happen. Show the total before payment, including fees. Name the thing being deleted and what goes with it. Say whether an email will be sent. Surprises after the fact, even good ones, erode trust.

## Reversibility

Wherever possible, make actions reversible: [undo](/breakdowns/undo-systems), a trash folder, a grace period before deletion. Reserve confirmation for what truly can't be undone, and make that confirmation specific.

## Honest feedback

Optimistic updates are fine when failure is rare and you admit it when it happens. Never show success for something that didn't succeed, and never hide errors to keep the interface clean. A clear "That didn't save, try again" builds more trust than a silent failure discovered later.

## Payment flows

Payment is where anxiety peaks. Keep the flow short, show progress, prevent double submission by disabling the button and showing that the payment is processing, and show a clear confirmation with what happens next. If something fails, say whether the user was charged.

## Consistency

Trust also comes from small things behaving the same way every time: buttons in the same place, the same words for the same actions, data that matches across screens. Inconsistency makes people wonder what else is wrong.
