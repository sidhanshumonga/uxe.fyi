Users do not experience milliseconds. They experience waiting. You can often make a product feel twice as fast without changing how long the work takes.

## Show the shape early

Skeleton screens prepare the eye for the layout that is coming, so the arrival of content feels like a fill-in rather than a jump. Use them for content with a predictable structure, not for every spinner.

<Demo name="skeleton-vs-spinner" caption="Switch between the two and notice where your eye goes." />

## Be optimistic

For actions that almost always succeed, such as likes, toggles and reorders, apply the result immediately and reconcile with the server in the background. Roll back visibly if it fails.

<Demo name="optimistic-ui" caption="Same click, two strategies: update instantly, or wait for the server." />

## Hide work

- Prefetch on hover or when a link enters the viewport
- Start uploads as soon as a file is chosen, not on submit
- Render the critical path first and defer the rest
